# Policy-constraints catalogue — will a lender wear what the documents show?

Generic, market-wide lending awareness. Not any one lender's policy and not credit advice: each finding is a heads-up with the evidence that resolves it, so problems surface before a lender raises them. Emit as `policy_flags[]` entries: `topic`, `observed` (with the actual figures), `why_it_matters`, `resolving_evidence`, `doc_ids`.

## Income

- **Short YTD (under ~3 months).** Annualised income is unreliable on a short YTD and many lenders will not use it without last-FY evidence. State the exact weeks held. Resolve: last-FY NOA / PAYG summary alongside, or wait for 3+ months YTD. Never annualise an ATO income statement by calendar days - STP YTD is payment-date based.
- **New employment / probation.** Start date under 6 months, or probation visible on a contract. Some lenders want probation complete, others accept with conditions. Resolve: employment letter confirming permanency, or note lender selection.
- **Casual income under 6-12 months in the role.** Most lenders want 6+ months casual history, some 12. Resolve: employment letter with start date, prior-year income evidence.
- **Overtime / bonus / commission / allowances relied on without history.** Variable income usually needs 12-24 months of evidence and is often shaded to 80%. Resolve: last FY payment summary or two years of evidence.
- **"Not tax ready" ATO income statement** treated as the income source. It is current-year interim evidence only. Resolve: tax-ready statement or NOA.
- **Second job under 6-12 months.** Same treatment as casual.

## Self-employed and alt-doc

- **ABN registered under 24 months, or GST under 6-12 months** where the deal is alt-doc: this fails the standard alt-doc gates the LM checklist itself states (GST registration + ABN 6+ months + 24 months industry experience). State the registration dates from the ABN search.
- **Declining profit year on year** across the two years of financials: lenders commonly use the lower year or average. Note the trend.
- **Add-backs doing the heavy lifting** (depreciation, one-offs): fine, but the credit officer should see the size of them.

## Liabilities and conduct

- **ATO debt or payment plan** on the NOA or portal extract: many lenders require it cleared or formally arranged; some cap at thresholds. Resolve: payment plan documents or payout evidence.
- **HECS/HELP visible on a payslip** but not disclosed as a liability: servicing impact via the repayment-income tiers. Resolve: confirm balance via ATO portal.
- **Credit card limits** aggregate: repayments are assessed near 3.8% of the limit per month regardless of balance. Note total limits when they are large against income.
- **SACC / payday lending** in the last 3-6 months: several lenders decline outright. Belongs in conduct flags too.

## Identity and residency

- **Visa conditions** on a visa grant or foreign passport: LVR caps and lender appetite vary; work rights and visa expiry matter. Resolve: visa grant notice, note for lender selection.
- **Recently expired ID** still in the pack: fine for the 2-ID count only if two others are valid; the address-bearing ID is usually the licence, so an expired licence often re-opens the address question.
- **Foreign income**: the LM checklist requires NAATI-translated payslips plus employer verification. If foreign-currency income appears, check that chain exists.

## Purchase mechanics

- **Gifted funds without a gift letter**; **deposit paid by a third party**; **funds to complete short** against the contract price plus duty (only when the numbers are visible in the docs - never estimate duty).
- **Settlement date near** (under ~3 weeks) with the checklist still incomplete: raise it as the first line of the credit officer notes.

Keep the list honest: only flag what the documents actually show, with the figures, and never speculate about a lender by name - the broker holds the lender relationship and the current policy.
